    Tropes¶

    Quote

    "I have to go into work. The Protestants are trying to kill my detectives."

    Andre (as Big Bill), summing up the premise on his way out of bed (Summer Service 06)

    PITF is an early-1990s network drama about the Paranatural Incident Task Force, a tiny FBI unit staffed by four people nobody else wanted: a true believer raised in a UFO church, a divorcing skeptic, a constipated con artist with a badge, and a Vietnam-era spook with drugs in his teeth. It is also, depending on which side of the screen you sit on, a Monster of the Week campaign run by Andre for Rita, Zack, Ezra and Dante, with a rotating bench of guest players. See The show for how seriously the table takes the framing (answer: there are credits).

    Across six arcs the team investigates a moth in Virginia, worms in Massachusetts, ape-men under a potato company in Georgia, body snatchers in Utah, a "Grey" in a Rhode Island attic, and then the moth again, because nothing in Radford ever stays dealt with. Along the way they fire one tactical nuclear weapon, lose rental cars at an alarming rate, and live through a 1992 election that Ross Perot wins.

    Somewhere in the middle there is also an anime. It has its own section.

    Examples below are sorted alphabetically and cited by episode. Beware of unmarked spoilers: this page gives away every twist the campaign has, including the one the players did not know about.

    A–C¶

    • Abandoned Mine: Dahlonega's tourist gold mine swallows a boy on a guided tour and leaves his Game Boy on the floor. Then the team falls through a boarded floor into a hidden shaft from the electric era, which is worse. (Appalachia 03)
    • Affably Evil: Jan Martense explains his ape-man breeding scheme with the cheerful candor of a man describing a kitchen remodel. (Walkers 01)
    • Agent Mulder: Andrew Howard, who keeps a mental list of UFO shapes and quizzes witnesses from it. (Radford Horror 04)
      • Inverted with Walt "Coyote" Murphy, whose explanation for a thing that fell burning out of the sky is ball lightning. Half an hour of Andrew's UFO case histories gets him as far as grudging respect. (Walkers 04)
    • Agent Scully: Huell Kendrick and the Biz start as the doubters. Huell holds out until he is hanging off an astronaut that something is lifting into the sky; the Biz is officially "on board" by Georgia. (Radford Horror 01, Radford Horror 05, Radford Horror 06, Appalachia 04)
      • Daniel Webster inherits the role in the final arc, and his notebook soon contains the line "Andrew Howard is a vampire?" (Radford Horrors 02)
    • Alien Abduction: Phoebe Augustine is taken, though not by tractor beam: something swoops down and lifts her. Years later Billy Thornton tells an abduction story nobody will listen to. (Radford Horror 04, Radford Horrors 04)
    • Alien Autopsy: The examiner opens a Ballard corpse and finds it is not human. (Walkers 08)
    • Aliens Steal Cattle: The Utah case opens with exploded sheep and mutilated cattle at the Sherman Ranch. The previous FBI team blamed lightning and drink. (Walkers 01, Walkers 02)
    • The Alleged Car: A proud lineage.
      • The task force's shabby 1961 Lincoln Continental. (Radford Horror 01)
      • Betsy, a loaner squad car that smells of "high school sadness" and comes with Cats costumes. (Walkers 04, Walkers 05, Walkers 08)
      • Aerial variant: Freak's house-painted Cessna "Annabelle", which has a disco ball, misfires, and barely clears a pine on takeoff. (Radford Horror 07)
    • Alternate History: Perot wins in 1992, fires the FBI director, and puts Rudy Giuliani in the chair. See Alternate history. (Roulet Estate 01, Radford Horrors 01)
    • Anachronism Stew: Averted with a vengeance. Andre fact-checks HIPAA, OxyContin, Tasers and the purple Game Boy mid-session, while Kurt says "rizz" in 1992 and gets called on it. (Radford Horror 02, Radford Horror 05, Appalachia 03, Radford Horrors 03)
    • Anticlimax: The Marblehead finale. The party nukes the boss from a mile away, finishes it with a flashlight when that is not enough, and declines to enter the facility. Andre admits the session ended early as a result. (Summer Service 06)
    • Anyone Can Die: Arnold Weathers and his dog Puddles are both worm hosts by the time anyone thinks to check on them. (Summer Service 05)
    • Apocalypse Cult: The Sons of Adam, with sea baptisms, a Revelation 13 sermon and a town council waiting for the second coming. (Summer Service 01, Summer Service 04)
    • Apocalypse Not: The skinwalkers' evacuation through the portal is foiled and the world is not destroyed. The community center is. (Walkers 09)
    • Bad Boss: Jan locks his own staff underground with the ape-men and leaves. (Appalachia 09)
    • Bag of Holding: Andrew's satchel, later formalized as the What I Need When I Need It bag, which produces a grenade at the worst possible moment in Utah. Andrew thinks he just forgets what he put in there. Andre's assessment of the mechanic: "the combinatorics are fucked." (Appalachia 07, Walkers 07)
      • Kurt's fanny pack and car trunk are the mundane version. They contain stilts. (Radford Horror 03, Summer Service 04)
    • Bait-and-Switch: Webster braces for the team's insane account of Radford and is handed a calm, mundane-sounding story about a spaceman. It is true. (Radford Horrors 04)
    • Bavarian Fire Drill: Henry is sent to Salem on a Vespa, in a storm, with Kurt's concealed carry permit as his credentials. (Summer Service 04)
      • The party bluffs its way past both stoned teenagers and police. (Walkers 04)
      • The Biz, a prisoner in Moscow, blends truth and exaggeration about how seriously the United States takes all this. (Radford Horrors 01)
    • Bears Are Bad News: The half-faced bear. Big Bill does not want to hear about the bears. (Appalachia 07)
    • Beware the Nice Ones: Ballard's friendly sheriff and friendly Walt, in a town with a great deal behind the friendliness. (Walkers 03)
    • Big Friendly Dog: Harvey the Great Dane adores Andrew, knocks him flat twice, and gets scooped up and cradled like a baby. (Appalachia 02, Appalachia 07)
    • Bigfoot, Sasquatch and Yeti: The whole Appalachian arc. The party spends weeks arguing real versus costumed, and the answer is "both". (Appalachia 01, Appalachia 03)
    • Bittersweet Ending: Two for two on arc finales.
      • Marblehead: the threat dies, a lot of people die with it, and there is fallout. (Summer Service 06)
      • Rhode Island: the case is solved, the team is shot up, scattered and disbanded. (Roulet Estate 03)
    • Bizarre Alien Biology: Per Wilbur, the Radford Moth has to breed, and its young are born pregnant. (Radford Horror 07)
    • Black Comedy: The young cop steps out of his squad car and a rifle shot takes off the top of his head, leaving his colleagues locked in the back seat and Kurt searching the body for the keys. (Roulet Estate 03)
    • Black Helicopter: Upgraded to silent black triangles, with hazmat teams underneath. (Walkers 09)
    • Body Horror: Pick an arc.
      • A partially eaten body by the interstate, and a proboscis that drains flesh and fat. (Radford Horror 04, Radford Horror 07)
      • Bloated, translucent, hairless worm bags, and Huell attempting self-surgery. (Summer Service 03, Summer Service 04, Summer Service 05)
      • The half-a-man crash survivor, tubed to a soldier. (Radford Horrors 04)

    • Brick Joke: In the first Marblehead episode Jester refuses Huell a toothbrush. In the last, Huell's death ray misses him and he knows exactly who is holding it. (Summer Service 01, Summer Service 06)

      Quote

      "It's you, Toothbrush Man!"

      Andre (as Jester) (Summer Service 06)

    • Burger Fool: The Spud Hut counter savant, a kid with perfect recall working a potato register. Kurt campaigns to get him a better job. The kid's position:

      Quote

      "Spud Hut saved my life."

      Andre (as the counter kid) (Appalachia 06)

    • By-the-Book Cop: Webster, clean-cut and shiny-shoed, dropped into this team. (Radford Horrors 01)

      • Big Bill demands clean hands in Georgia. He does not get them. (Appalachia 03)
    • The Call Is Coming from Inside the House: The Roulet intruder has been living in the owners' attic for a year, photographing them asleep. (Roulet Estate 02)
    • Came Back Wrong: Andrew is shot point-blank, wakes under a pile of bodies in a morgue, and comes back fast, bulletproof and thirsty. See Andrew Howard (the Monstrous). (Walkers 08, Walkers 09)
    • Car Fu: Kurt hits Agent Murphy with a borrowed car. By Utah, Andre just asks whether he wants to hit them. (Radford Horror 03, Walkers 08)
    • Cassandra Truth: Practically a recurring cast member.
      • Andrew's sea-monster guess is brushed off; Madeline Weathers's report was ignored. (Summer Service 01, Summer Service 05)
      • Edward Nick saw something real and his mother filed it under "Bigfoot nonsense". (Appalachia 02)
      • Alberta Wilmarth was ignored by everyone until the FBI arrived. (Walkers 02)
      • The party truthfully warns Ballard about the water and Ballard hears a joke. (Walkers 03)
      • Kurt's portal warnings, post-disbandment, reach nobody. (Roulet Estate 03)

    • Catchphrase: Andrew, who believes in aliens, angels and the Masters, has one firm line. (Walkers 01, Walkers 06)

      Quote

      "Oh, ghosts aren't real."

      Rita (as Andrew) (Walkers 01)

    • Chekhov's Armoury: The basement arsenal under the D.C. barbershop, and whatever is in Kurt's trunk this week. (Radford Horror 01, Summer Service 06)

    • Chekhov's Gun: Nearly every episode loads one. Highlights:
      • The bug in Dr. Lori's vent and the pink butterfly hair tie. (Radford Horror 02, Radford Horror 03, Radford Horror 05)
      • Everclear, bolt cutters and a tinfoil-lined welding mask, all packed before the church and all used in it. (Radford Horror 07)
      • The Davy Crockett, carried for three sessions before it is fired. (Summer Service 04, Summer Service 06)
      • Kurt's Claymore, carried since the previous mystery. (Appalachia 08)
      • Kurt's hollow molars. The LSD one is first spat into Agent Murphy's mouth in Radford, and its twin is bitten on purpose in Utah. (Radford Horror 03, Walkers 05)
      • The beta blockers from the attic lair, which resurface with the gunman. (Roulet Estate 03)
    • Chew Toy: Andrew is berated by Big Bill, mistrusted by witnesses, airsick, bitten by an old woman, and eventually murdered and replaced. (Radford Horror 07, Appalachia 07, Walkers 08, Roulet Estate 03)
    • Cliffhanger: Andre ends on them deliberately.
      • Molly Underhill reveals a mutilated body. (Radford Horror 04)
      • Soldiers and flamethrower men close in on a half-dead party. (Radford Horror 07)
      • The session stops with the Ivermectin unopened and the team hunted. (Summer Service 05)
      • The Appalachia recordings end unresolved; the finale itself is one of the lost episodes. (Appalachia 09)
      • Giuliani closes the premiere of the last arc by asking about Radford. (Radford Horrors 01)
    • Clingy MacGuffin: The folded face, which travels in a bag. Later arcs still ask "Is there a face this time?" (Appalachia 04, Radford Horrors 04)
    • Closed Circle: Three flavors.
      • Tropical Storm Andrew 2 isolates Marblehead and grounds the cavalry. (Summer Service 05)
      • Phones, power, cars and the road are all cut around Ballard and Roosevelt. (Walkers 07, Walkers 08)
      • The Roulet House: snow, no car, no phone, no power. (Roulet Estate 01, Roulet Estate 02)
    • Cloudcuckoolander: Rutherford, night clerk and Anunnaki theorist. (Radford Horror 04)
      • Robert Bigelow, and Drebin with his iguana. (Walkers 05)
      • Eduardo Hooper, the drunk who orders off the secret menu. (Appalachia 07)
    • Cold Open: Nearly every episode has one, usually ending on Andre saying "cut to credits". The player characters are often nowhere in them.     Tropes for Philosopher's Fist 3.J/2: Unchainable¶

    Non-canon

    Philosopher's Fist 3.J/2: Unchainable is a one-shot in a different rules system, framed as a 1992 OVA airing on television inside the PITF fiction. Nothing in this section happened to the task force. All examples are from the one-shot.

    • Big Eater: Derk hands a vendor $100 by mistake and receives 98 hot dog tacos and $2 change.
    • Cliffhanger: Derk comes down in the ocean and is last seen outside a McDonald's in Wisukonshin, spending his last two dollars on a call home.
    • Cloudcuckoolander's Minder: Mirabeth, in theory.
    • Con Man: Mirabeth invents an essential-oil MLM, the oil pyramid, and takes a $400 down payment from a mother with a sobbing son.
    • Cool Old Lady: A glamorous senior martial artist who speed-walks an interstate in pumps behind a parasol.
    • Daddy Issues: Ovain and Derk both have them. Derk's attempt to bond over it is a miss.

    • Destructive Savior: Derk saves the president and causes $125,000 in property damage, a ruptured gas line and four evacuated blocks.

      Quote

      "A natural disaster with legs and a mouth."

      Andre (as Colonel Sparago), on Derk

    • Dumb Muscle: Derk. Andre: "it's amazing how Derk being fucking stupid has dominated the tone of this one-shot."

    • Fish out of Water: A feral alien prince in a world of libraries and Motel 6s.
    • Gentle Giant: He gives piggyback rides and wants the stone to resurrect his friend Fatty Gibbs.
    • Kamehame Hadoken: Ovain's white fire and ki jets.
    • Literal-Minded: Told to use his inside voice, Derk speaks with a hand over his mouth for the rest of the scene.
    • Malaproper: "Asteroid" for "steroid", every time.
    • Mentor Archetype: Mirabeth. Rita picked the option where she "was supposed to have mentored somebody and now it's embarrassing."
    • Nigh-Invulnerability: A groin punch lands on Derk with a metallic clang.
    • Our Presidents Are Different: The Werewolf President, a genial man who agrees privately that turning into a dog is cool.
    • Power Incontinence: Derk cannot aim a jump. One ends in space.
    • Rule of Funny: The governing law of the planet.
    • Show Within a Show: See the note above.
    • Super-Speed: Mirabeth's legendary pace.
    • Sympathetic Villain: Ovain is a shaky former conqueror in therapy whose online dating went badly. He leaves the planet "and its Tinder".
    • Tournament Arc: Promised by the World Martial Arts Tournament and then subverted when the whole thing is suspended over a judge on steroids before a single bout.
    • Training from Hell: Fourteen years alone on a high-gravity planet, eating scorpions.

Rita picked Andrew's Spud Hut order by rolling a d100 against the menu, which Andre says was AI-generated with human tuning. (Radford Horror 07, Appalachia 02, Appalachia 04, Walkers 08)
    • Troubled Production: Krisp noise suppression eats yells, a creature's cry and Zack's Geiger-counter impression. Discord breaks the credit music, the dice bot breaks too, and Andre rolls for the players. (Radford Horror 07, Summer Service 06, Radford Horrors 03)
