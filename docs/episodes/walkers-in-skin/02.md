# The Walkers in Skin, Episode 02

The task force flies to Utah, is briefed by the Salt Lake City FBI office, and moves into a double-wide on Sherman Ranch, where it meets a gate guard, a goose and a billionaire.

## Synopsis

With [Big Bill](../../characters/big-bill.md) glad to have them far from any camera, the team flies to Salt Lake City and collects a case file from two local FBI agents who blame the ranch reports on lightning and drink. At [Sherman Ranch](../../locations/sherman-ranch.md) they meet the gate guard [Walt](../../characters/walt-murphy.md), their client [Alberta Wilmarth](../../characters/alberta-wilmarth.md), her goose [Nancy](../../characters/nancy-the-goose.md), and the tenant [Robert Bigelow](../../characters/robert-bigelow.md), whose instruments record heat and electrical anomalies that stop half a mile from the ranch. A grocery run into Ballard turns up resentment of the town's leaders and their new community center. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

## Plot

### Out of the public eye

Big Bill, distracted by the election, sets one condition: stop at the [FBI Salt Lake City field office](../../organizations/fbi-salt-lake-city-field-office.md) for the files on earlier reports and work with the local agents. The team flies, which costs [Kurt Larson](../../characters/kurt-larson.md) his Claymores but not his licensed [P90](../../items/kurts-p90.md). [Andrew Howard](../../characters/andrew-howard.md) books the rental, a 1990 [Plymouth Voyager](../../items/plymouth-voyager.md) with fake-wood paneling. Dante's 2d6 flight roll is a six: a toddler kicks the seat of [the Biz](../../characters/dick-baxter.md) every 25 seconds, and a few passengers double-take at one of America's most hated people. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

### The Salt Lake City briefing

In a second-floor conference room the team meets [Carl Gardner](../../characters/carl-gardner.md), short, hearty and full of "big cop energy", and his tall, silent partner [Philip Bishop](../../characters/minor-walkers.md). Kurt introduces himself as CIA, [UTAW](../../organizations/utaw.md) division; Gardner knows "those boys, explode the cigars and all that." Gardner's rundown of the ranch: a Native American former Army Ranger on the billionaire's security staff says he shot a snooper by the barn, though Gardner found no physical evidence; a young woman reports seeing her dead mother, and there is no twin; cattle mutilations, put down to lightning; lights and lost time, put down to drinking. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

Kurt rolls Investigate a Mystery on the room's string-covered corkboard and gets a 9. The board is another case entirely: unacceptable levels of semen in public drinking fountains, the start of the [Ballard water contamination](../../lore/ballard-water-contamination.md) thread. While Gardner goes to look up the billionaire's name, [Huell Kendrick](../../characters/huell-kendrick.md) tries "So— Mormons, am I right?" on Bishop, and four minutes of sweaty silence follow. Gardner returns with the name of the billionaire who "skeeves me the hell out": Robert Bigelow, a real estate baron whose security man fired the shot. He hands over a copy of the file, recognizes the Biz as "the fella who spied on Clinton" ("Had it coming"), recommends funeral potatoes, and tells them to say they are "a friend of Bonaparte" for 20% off gas. Rita tests whether Andrew's appearance alone earns the discount; the receipt shows it did. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

### Sherman Ranch

After a drive of almost three hours the team reaches Ballard, a town of a few hundred with a sheriff's station in a double-wide, and then the ranch gate. Walt, the guard, is the man who fired at the snooper: he says he gave a warning first and the figure came at him "at pretty high speed". He came back from the Gulf War about a year ago. He asks whether they have come about "the sugar heist", a box truck of cane sugar stolen from a truck stop three or four weeks ago. He calls the ranch "haunted as hell": sheep that burst ("You ever microwaved an egg?"), cattle simply dead (see [livestock mutilations](../../lore/livestock-mutilations.md)). *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

Walt leads them to the [guest double-wide](../../locations/guest-double-wide.md) and is promptly attacked and bitten by Nancy, an enormous goose. He explains the house (furnace on a switch, keys under a ceramic frog, a back door with only a deadbolt) and the rules: headlights on at night, and [the big house](../../locations/big-house.md) and its barn are off-limits. From the window he points out the white [community center](../../locations/community-center.md) and its gray [substation](../../locations/substation.md), built four miles out of town and in sight of the ranch. Some of the town's leaders, he says, "make my skin crawl". *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

At the pig pen Alberta welcomes Andrew and admits she "genuinely did not expect to get traction". She makes Walt confess that in the summer of 1986 he tried to get people to call him "Coyote"; Andrew and Kurt adopt the name at once. Something pulled up a couple of fence posts overnight and Walt's dog was cagey, though the animal headcount was fine. Alberta has seen no lights, but the ranch always hums at night, which sets Andrew visibly vibrating. Nancy runs at Kurt at full speed, jumps into Alberta's arms and hisses at him. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

### Bigelow

At the big house a blonde guard with a ponytail and a hip holster, the [Ponytail Lady](../../characters/ponytail-lady.md), asks them to leave their weapons at the door. Kurt says no, so the meeting happens on the porch over sweet tea and buttered bread. Bigelow, a sour-faced middle-aged man in an expensive suit, calls Alberta "Madeline" and corrects himself. He has read up on the team ("a lot of interesting stories follow in your wake"), and the Clinton affair "didn't even make the list". [His operation](../../organizations/bigelows-research-operation.md) logs electromagnetic, thermal and acoustic readings on [tape reels](../../items/bigelows-recordings.md): many unexplained heat signatures and electrical events, all within the ranch and half a mile around it, none in town. He dismisses the substation as unrelated. He already knows about the contamination in Ballard's water and adds that it is high in arsenic; the big house filters its own. He stands by Walt's judgment over the shooting, is asked about the team joining a night patrol, and leaves for a satellite-phone call. The guard gives the house rules: keep Bigelow happy, and keep the goose happy. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

### Ballard

At the [general store](../../locations/ballard-general-store.md) a lifelong resident with an enormous beehive hairdo sells them bologna, white bread and cheap beer for $35.25. Andrew's awkward questioning gets results anyway: the ranch is weird, "but that's the county"; she resents the community center, blames the mayor ([Wilford Hiram](../../characters/wilford-hiram.md)) and "the goddamn busybody" [Deborah Wells](../../characters/deborah-wells.md), and would rather have a clinic. The team tells her to drink only bottled or well water. On the drive back Kurt informs Andrew that he has no game. The night ends with Kurt taking the toddler's bedroom and test-flushing a toilet that gurgles long and anemically. *([Walkers 02](02.md))*

## Cold open and framing

No player characters appear. At night on a desert ranch, a Native American man in a big cowboy hat arrives with a shotgun and a large dog, which growls softly into the dark. He finds nothing and drives off. A coyote steps out from behind the sheep, trots toward the edge of the pasture, and stands up on two legs. Cut to credits. Ezra: "Regular coyote." Andre: "Yeah, just normal." See [upright coyotes](../../monsters/upright-coyotes.md) and [The show](../../lore/the-show.md). *([Walkers 02](02.md))*
